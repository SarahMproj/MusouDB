# Intellectual Property and Licensing Strategy

> This document is a product-design policy, not legal advice.

## Objective

Build a valuable community and technology platform that can operate as an unofficial project while remaining structurally ready for a future license or sanctioned partnership.

## Brand separation

MusouDB must not imply that it is an official KOEI TECMO product.

Required practices:

- Use original MusouDB branding and interface design
- Display a clear unofficial-project disclaimer
- Use publisher and game names only as factual references
- Avoid official logos, trade dress, and confusingly similar branding
- Do not describe events as official, sanctioned, or world championships without written authority

A separate hosted-company brand may eventually sit above the open-source MusouDB data project if trademark review recommends it.

## Repository asset policy

The public repository must not include:

- Extracted or decrypted game files
- Character models, textures, sprites, maps, or UI assets copied from games
- Music, sound effects, or voice recordings
- Official promotional art without documented permission
- Copied guide, wiki, or database text
- Rehosted gameplay recordings
- Circumvention tools or instructions intended to defeat access controls

Acceptable contributions may include:

- Original schemas and software
- Factual identifiers and community-authored metadata
- Original summaries and analysis
- Public-domain historical information with sources
- Original or properly licensed community artwork
- External links to player-controlled evidence, subject to policy

## Data provenance

Every accepted data contribution should identify its provenance:

- `community_observation`
- `official_public_source`
- `public_domain_history`
- `contributor_original`

The project should avoid importing large third-party datasets until their licenses and extraction methods are reviewed.

## Gameplay evidence

Initial challenge systems should store external URLs and submission metadata rather than copying video files.

Before commercial launch, counsel should review:

- Whether embedding or thumbnailing evidence is permitted
- Publisher-specific video policies
- Copyright takedown operations
- Repeat-infringer procedures
- Rights granted by users in the Terms of Service

Users must represent that they control or are authorized to submit the linked footage.

## Licensing pathway

A publisher approach should request narrowly scoped rights tied to a specific pilot rather than seeking every possible right immediately.

Potential first request:

- Permission to operate a named seasonal community challenge
- Limited use of approved marks and promotional assets
- Approved terminology and disclaimer language
- Permission to feature qualifying gameplay evidence
- A defined territory, term, and set of games
- Optional co-promotion and prize support

Later rights may include official profile badges, telemetry access, creator programs, live events, merchandise, broadcast packages, and additional franchises.

## Partnership posture

A licensing proposal should demonstrate:

- An active, measurable community
- Strong moderation and safety systems
- Respectful handling of publisher-owned material
- Reliable challenge operations
- Incremental player engagement and game discovery
- Privacy-safe, aggregate audience insight
- A controlled and reversible pilot

MusouDB should never present acquisition by a publisher as the expected outcome. The company should create standalone value through community infrastructure, data organization, event operations, and verification technology.

## Before monetization

Complete professional review of:

- Brand and trademark clearance
- Terms of Service and Privacy Policy
- DMCA agent and takedown process where applicable
- Publisher video and fan-content policies
- Youth safety and age-gating
- Contest and prize rules
- Sponsorship disclosures
- Data licensing

## Takedown response

The project should maintain a documented, respectful escalation process:

1. Preserve the request and identify the affected material.
2. Temporarily restrict disputed material when appropriate.
3. Review provenance and applicable permissions.
4. Respond through one designated contact.
5. Remove or replace material when required.
6. Record the outcome and update contributor guidance.

Do not encourage contributors to evade legitimate rights-holder requests.
